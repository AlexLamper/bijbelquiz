## Notes and doubts

- John 20: the chapter never names its writer, so question 10 asks what "the writer" says, not John.
- John 20 q7: distractors avoid other sayings of Jesus in this chapter ("Peace be to you" is said in
  verses 19, 21 and 26, so the question is tied to verse 19, the first time behind locked doors).
- John 21: Peter's three answers all use "I have affection for you" in this text; q8 and q9 are tied
  to the first and the third exchange so that only one answer fits.
- Acts 1 q10: the other candidate carries three names in verse 23 (Joseph / Barsabbas / Justus), so
  the distractors use apostles named in verse 13 instead, to avoid three names for one man.
- Acts 2 q9: the text reads "in the name of Jesus Christ"; the answer shortens this to "in Jesus'
  name" to keep the option lengths even. Nothing is added.
- Acts 5 q9: Gamaliel is named in verse 34; "Judas of Galilee" (verse 37) is used as a distractor
  because the text mentions him only as an earlier rebel.
- Acts 6 q9-q10: Stephen is named in verses 5, 8 and 9, so he is named in the questions although
  verses 11 and 15 only say "him".
- Acts 7 is Stephen's speech; every question is about what the speech itself says, not about the
  Old Testament accounts behind it. Stephen is named because verse 59 names him.
- Acts 8: verse 37 is empty in this text, so nothing is asked from it; q10 uses verse 40.
- Acts 9 q9-q10: Aeneas (Lydda) and Tabitha (Joppa) appear in the same chapter, so each question
  names the town to keep the other person plainly wrong.
- Acts 10 q5: the answers are written as statements rather than quotes, because the verbatim quote
  ("anything that is common or unclean") would be far longer than the distractors.
- No chapter in this batch was too thin for 10 questions.
